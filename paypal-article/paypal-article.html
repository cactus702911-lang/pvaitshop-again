What Is PayPal and Why Is It So Widely Used?

PayPal was founded in 1998 and has since grown into one of the most recognized financial technology companies in the world. Today, it operates in over 200 countries and regions, supports transactions in more than 25 currencies, and processes billions of dollars in payments every single year.

Unlike traditional bank transfers, PayPal offers near-instant payment processing, a globally recognized trust layer for buyers and sellers, and direct integrations with the world's largest e-commerce platforms. According to PayPal's official newsroom, the platform had over 400 million active accounts as of early 2024, and processes an average of over 55 million transactions every single day.

For freelancers, online sellers, digital agencies, and international businesses, PayPal is not just convenient — it is often the primary payment infrastructure for their entire operation. Clients on platforms like Upwork, Fiverr, Toptal, eBay, Etsy, and Shopify routinely request PayPal as their preferred payment or payout method.

📊 Key Statistic

PayPal handles over $1.53 trillion in Total Payment Volume annually (as of 2023 annual report). It remains the #1 digital wallet globally by active user count, ahead of Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Venmo in global merchant acceptance.

Despite its scale and reach, PayPal's identity verification process — known internally as Know Your Customer (KYC) — has become a significant barrier for users in certain countries. Document rejections, delayed reviews, and regional banking limitations mean that millions of people who want a fully functional account cannot access one quickly through the standard process.

Verified vs. Unverified PayPal: The Real Difference

PayPal distinguishes between two core account states. Understanding the difference is essential before making any financial decisions tied to the platform.

🔓 Unverified PayPal Account

An unverified account is one where the user has completed basic registration — providing an email, phone number, and basic personal details — but has not completed PayPal's full identity verification process. Unverified accounts face:

  • Strict sending and withdrawal limits (often capped at a few hundred dollars total)
  • No access to PayPal Seller Protection
  • Inability to link a bank account for direct withdrawal in many cases
  • Risk of account limitation if transaction volumes exceed the unverified threshold
  • Ineligibility for PayPal's business merchant tools, including invoicing

✅ Verified PayPal Account

A verified account has completed PayPal's full KYC review process, which typically includes submitting a government-issued ID (passport or national ID), proof of address, and linking a confirmed bank account or credit card. According to PayPal's official account limits documentation, verified accounts enjoy:

  • Significantly higher or unlimited sending and receiving thresholds
  • Full bank withdrawal access from day one
  • Eligibility for Seller Protection on eligible transactions
  • Access to PayPal's business invoicing tools
  • Higher internal trust score — reducing the risk of automated limitation flags
⚠️

Important Note: PayPal's verification requirements and limits vary by country. Users in certain regions may face additional document requirements or regional restrictions. Best Practices: Keeping Your PayPal Account Safe After Delivery

To maintain long-term account stability and reduce the risk of automated flags, follow these best practices immediately after receiving your credentials:

  • Change your password immediately — update to a strong, unique password that you have not used anywhere else. Use a password manager if possible.
  • Update recovery email and phone number — change the recovery contact details to your own before making any transactions.
  • Link your bank account gradually — if the account is new, allow a short settling period before making high-volume transactions. For aged accounts, normal usage can begin immediately.
  • Use the account from a consistent IP location — frequent location changes or logins from multiple countries in a short period can trigger PayPal's automated security systems. Use the account from a stable IP environment.
  • Avoid mass-volume sending in the first days — even verified accounts can trigger automated reviews if large transactions occur immediately. Warm up the account with moderate activity first.
  • Enable two-factor authentication — PayPal supports 2FA via SMS or authenticator app. Enabling this adds an important security layer to your account.
  • Keep transaction descriptions clean and professional — use clear, legitimate transaction notes for every payment sent or received. Vague or misleading payment descriptions increase the likelihood of PayPal flagging a transaction for manual review.

For comprehensive security guidance directly from PayPal, see PayPal's Security Center.
